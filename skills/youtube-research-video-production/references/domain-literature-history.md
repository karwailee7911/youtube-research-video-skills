# Literature, philosophy, and history evidence rules

Use this file only for literature, philosophy, intellectual history, or historical topics.

## Source order

Prefer:

1. Named editions, manuscripts, archival records, letters, speeches, and primary documents
2. Scholarly editions, translations, and critical apparatus
3. Peer-reviewed scholarship and reputable academic reference works
4. Later retellings only as evidence of reception

Record author, work, edition, translator or editor, publication date, volume, page, chapter, and textual variant. Preserve the full passage around a quotation. Distinguish the author's wording from a character's speech and from the creator's interpretation.

## Required distinctions

- Primary text versus later paraphrase
- Textual fact versus interpretation
- Authorial context versus modern application
- Original language versus translation choice
- Composition date versus publication or reception date
- Historical evidence versus generated reconstruction

Mark disputed attribution, uncertain dating, missing context, and translation ambiguity. Label generated scenes as illustration or reconstruction. Avoid presenting a single critical reading as the only accepted meaning.
